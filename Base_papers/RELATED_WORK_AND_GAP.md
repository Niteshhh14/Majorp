# Direct related work and project gap

Research check updated 2026-10-30 after inspection of the complete supplied AECRN paper. This note selects a defensible comparison; it distinguishes a paper-inspired adaptation from a faithful reproduction.

## What our code currently does

- Classical reference: spectral subtraction (`alpha=1.15`, `floorFactor=0.04`) followed by the MATLAB CVBW audiogram preset.
- Neural pilot: a small profile-independent STFT magnitude-mask CNN trained to denoise VCTK/DEMAND mixtures using waveform/spectral/SI-SDR losses, then the same MATLAB CVBW stage is applied afterward.
- It does **not** currently take an audiogram as a neural input, predict NAL-NL2 compensated clean speech, or jointly optimize the denoiser and fitting stage. The selected model was tuned with pre-CVBW metrics.
- The 32-case validation-only aided audit gives +4.887 dB mean paired aided SI-SDR change for neural versus +1.566 dB classical; neural is higher on 19/32. Profile 4 reverses the trend (neural minus classical −1.454 dB; 1/8 cases higher). Paired aided STOI is finite for 30 cases; the neural-minus-classical change averages +0.0556. These are exploratory VCTK/DEMAND validation results, not a test-set result or clinical claim.

## Full-text findings and reproduction decision (2026-10-30)

The supplied paper describes two distinct evaluations. Its ideal-benefit analysis uses 60-second ISTS with stationary speech-shaped noise at -15/0/15 dB and standard N2/N3/N4 audiograms. The learned-model evaluation uses 32 kHz VoiceBank+DEMAND: 11,572 paired training examples, 28 training speakers, 10 noise types and -15/-10/-5/0 dB; the test has 824 pairs and two held-out speakers across five noises. The method evaluates 50 audiograms and trains using 500 synthetic audiograms between N3 and N4. Training is described as 100 epochs, batch 16, Adam at 0.001, spectral MSE, 10 ms STFT window, and validation-based checkpoint choice. Reported metrics are HASPI, HASQI, PESQ, and STOI. The paper reports its own Table II values: Noisy .20/.14/1.28/.68; OpenMHA .28/.22/1.34/.57; CRN+NAL-NL2 .32/.25/1.44/.76; AECRN .53/.37/1.69/.82 (HASPI/HASQI/PESQ/STOI). These are paper-reported results under its protocol, not our results.

The paper's baseline includes OpenMHA noise reduction and a CRN followed by NAL-NL2; it describes NAL-NL2 via an OpenMHA wrapper/proprietary prescription library. We have not verified access to the required NAL-NL2 software or generated targets. The architecture figure clarifies the three audiogram-conditioned channel embeddings, but the paper does not give all layer widths/kernel dimensions needed to guarantee bit-for-bit architecture reproduction. Therefore do not call a new model an AECRN reproduction. An implementable and clearly labeled adaptation can condition a compact model on our six-frequency audiogram vector and train toward the project's clean-through-CVBW target, with target generation held identical to the classical cascade. This changes the prescription target and sample rate, and must be called “AECRN-inspired CVBW-conditioned model,” not AECRN.

Before that model experiment, settle a focused validation protocol: fixed paired mixtures, profile-conditioned target audio, identical speaker splits and aided references, profile-stratified STOI/SI-SDR plus validated HASPI/HASQI if available, and model parameters/latency. Tune only on validation speakers, include profile 4 as a preregistered weak-profile analysis, and preserve test speakers. The existing profile-independent pilot's 32-case aided audit is exploratory; it was selected with pre-CVBW metrics and has pronounced profile dependence. Do not transfer Table II values across datasets or treat more profile conditions alone as novelty.

## Defensible next study

Do not pitch “deep learning + audiogram fitting” as the novelty. Run a controlled, same-data comparison with:

1. Current classical spectral subtraction + CVBW.
2. Current profile-independent neural denoiser + the same CVBW.
3. An explicitly labeled AECRN-inspired audiogram-conditioned direct compensated-speech model, trained against project CVBW targets (unless NAL-NL2 software and exact method settings become available).
4. A smaller/cheaper variant of that model, if the full comparator establishes an appropriate quality-versus-computation target.

Keep speech speakers disjoint across train/validation/test. Use common noisy mixtures and the same audiogram profiles for every method. Report per-case and aggregate SI-SDR plus hearing-loss-aware quality/intelligibility metrics (HASQI/HASPI where valid implementations are available), missing-metric counts, parameter count, multiply-accumulate estimate, and measured runtime. Analyze profile 4 separately because the current pilot is weak there. Select architecture and settings on validation only; evaluate held-out speakers once after freezing the method.

Potential contribution, conditional on evidence: a lower-compute audiogram-conditioned enhancement method that preserves or improves aided intelligibility/quality across audiograms and SNRs. This is a hypothesis, not yet a novelty claim. If reproducing AECRN requires unavailable method details, data or prescription software, narrow the project to a transparent benchmark/ablation study and ask the guide to approve that scope rather than claiming to beat AECRN with non-matched scores. Claims about user benefit require human listening validation; objective simulations alone cannot support clinical effectiveness.
